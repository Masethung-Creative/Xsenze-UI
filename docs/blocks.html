Blocks for landing pages

The parts that make a product page: a hero with a rolling word, a flow diagram, logo rows, live demos and a closing band. Each part is a normal Xsenze component, ready to drop into a page section by section.

What uses what

  • Hero holds an Eyebrow, a title with a Rotator and its caret, buttons, a Flow and a Command with Copy and Beam, over a Dot field. Its parts come in with Reveal.
  • Flow joins a card with an avatar and a Checklist (the source), Layers and a small Command (the hub), and Tiles (the targets).
  • Feature row holds Facts, and a visual such as a Window with Compare or Ledger played by a Sequence, or a card of Marquees.
  • Band wraps every section; Curtain opens it, and data-theme gives it its own theme.
  • Overlay navbar carries the Motion button and the theme button, and reads the theme of the Band beneath it.

Scroll progress

A thin bar in the brand colour across the top of the screen that fills as the page is read. Use one per page. Scroll inside the preview and watch the line at the very top.

Motion

Every moving part here follows three rules: it stops when the system asks for less motion, it stops when the page's motion button is off, and the content is complete without it.

Dot field

A fine grid of dots that drifts slowly behind a section, stays quiet behind the headline, and lights up in the brand colour around the pointer.

Move the pointer over this area

Put it as the first child of a section with position: relative and isolation: isolate (bands and the hero have both). Tune it with --xz-dot-gap (16px), --xz-dot-size (1px) and --xz-dotfield-quiet (how far down the quiet area sits, 30%). --even drops the quiet area; --fine packs the dots closer. It is hidden from screen readers and ignores touch.

Layers

A stack of plates seen from above at an angle. A wave of light rises through the stack, plate by plate, and the stack leans towards the pointer. Use it as the hub of a Flow or as a hero picture.

Options: data-xz-layers="5" plates (2 to 12), --xz-layers-size (10rem), --xz-layers-gap (0.85rem), and content in xz-layers__face on the top plate. The nearest data-xz-tilt-area, or else the section, tracks the pointer. With an aria-label it is announced as a picture; without one it is hidden as decoration.

Integration

  • Files: nothing new to load. The blocks are inside xsenze.min.css and xsenze.min.js; together they add about 4.6 KB of CSS and 5.6 KB of JavaScript, gzipped.
  • Start: everything starts from the markup when the page loads. After adding markup later, call Xsenze.init(element).
  • Page set-up: one data-xz-scroll-progress bar and one Motion button per page. Pages with an overlay navbar need the hero (or 3.5rem of padding) first.
  • Themes: any section can take data-theme="dark" or "light". The overlay navbar with adapt follows the band underneath.
  • Source: the blocks live in src/blocks/, one folder per kind: navigation, hero, content, forms, data-display, footer and motion.

Not included, on purpose

  • Real 3D models. The kit has no dependencies and no 3D engine, so Layers draws a stack of plates in CSS instead. For a true 3D object, add your own renderer inside xz-flow__hub.
  • Particle effects drawn on the graphics card. Dot field gives a similar moving texture with CSS, at a fraction of the cost, and works everywhere.
  • Smoothed scrolling. Taking over the mouse wheel makes pages feel slow and fights screen readers and keyboards, so scrolling stays native. Depth and Curtain follow it.
  • A display typeface just for headlines. Headings use the kit's display font, so a page stays consistent with the rest of the product.
  • Many-coloured highlights. Beam, routes and progress use the brand orange only, as everywhere else in the kit.
  • Holding the page blank until an animation is ready. Hero parts reveal with CSS as the page opens, so the headline is visible at once, even if the script fails.
